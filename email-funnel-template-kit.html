Quick-Start Guide

Get Your First Funnel Live

Follow these steps to go from template to active automation. The whole process should take 30–60 minutes for your first funnel.

  1. Choose your funnel Use the Funnel Selection Guide on the next page to pick the sequence that matches your current goal. If you're brand new, start with the Welcome Series.
  2. Identify your audience Before writing a word, be clear on who you're talking to. Use the Funnel Planner worksheet to capture your audience description, their core problem, and the result they want.
  3. Replace all placeholders Go through each email and replace every [bracketed placeholder] with your specific information. Use the Placeholder Guide above to know exactly what each one means.
  4. Add your links Update every URL — your product page, booking link, website, and any resource links. Test every link before activating the sequence.
  5. Customize the copy The templates give you a strong foundation, but make them sound like you. Adjust the tone, add personal stories, and change any examples to fit your industry.
  6. Add your branding In your email platform, apply your brand colors, logo, font, and email footer. Consistent branding builds trust and recognition.
  7. Copy emails into your platform Paste each email into a new automation sequence in your chosen email platform (AWeber, Mailchimp, Kit, Brevo, ActiveCampaign, HubSpot, or other).
  8. Set the timing Use the "Recommended Timing" shown on each template to set your automation delays. Adjust if needed based on your audience's typical behavior.
  9. Send yourself a test email Trigger the sequence for your own email address and check every email carefully. Read it on mobile and desktop.
  10. Check every link Click every single link in every email — from your inbox, not the editor. Broken links in a live sequence are difficult to undo once subscribers have received them.
  11. Activate the automation Once you're satisfied, set the automation to live. Double-check the trigger (e.g., on form signup, on tag added, on purchase) before going live.
  12. Monitor your results Check open rates and click rates after the first 7–14 days. Use the Metrics Cheat Sheet in Bonus 6 to understand what the numbers mean and what to look for.

Email Pre-Send Checklist

Run through this checklist before activating any email automation or sending any broadcast. Print it out or work through it on screen before every send.

  • Subject line reviewed — Does it accurately represent the email content? Would you open it?
  • Preview text set — Is the preview text filled in? Does it complement (not repeat) the subject line?
  • All placeholders replaced — Search for "[" in the email editor. Every bracket should be gone.
  • Every link tested — Click every single link in the email from your inbox (not the editor). Do they all work?
  • CTA is clear and prominent — Is it obvious what you want the reader to do next?
  • Spelling and grammar checked — Read the email aloud. Does it sound like a real person wrote it?
  • Mobile appearance checked — Preview the email on a mobile view. Is it readable and well-spaced?
  • Images checked (if applicable) — Do all images load? Do they have alt text in case they're blocked?
  • Unsubscribe option present — Is there a working unsubscribe link in the email footer? (Required by law in most countries.)
  • Sender name correct — Does the "From" name match what subscribers expect? Use a consistent name.
  • Test email sent and reviewed — Did you send a test to yourself and read it carefully in your actual inbox?
  • Automation timing correct — Are the delays set correctly? Does the sequence flow as intended?
  • Trigger condition verified — Is the automation set to fire on the right trigger (form submit, tag, purchase, etc.)?
  • Final read-through complete — Read the whole email one more time as if you're a subscriber receiving it for the first time.

Email Marketing Metrics Cheat Sheet

A plain-English guide to the numbers that matter. Use this to understand what your email platform is telling you — and what to focus on when results aren't where you want them.

Open Rate

What it means: The percentage of delivered emails that were opened. Most platforms calculate this as (unique opens ÷ delivered emails) × 100.

Why it matters: Open rate tells you how compelling your subject lines are and how much your subscribers trust you enough to open your emails. A declining open rate is often an early warning sign of list fatigue or deliverability issues.

Watch for

Sudden drops may indicate deliverability problems or subject line fatigue. Gradual declines usually mean the list needs re-engagement or content improvement.

Improve it by

Writing more specific, curiosity-driven subject lines. Sending at times when your audience is more likely to check email. Cleaning your list of inactive subscribers.

Note

Apple Mail Privacy Protection (MPP) inflates open rates for Apple Mail users. Many platforms now show "adjusted" open rates — use click rate as a more reliable engagement signal.

Click-Through Rate (CTR)

What it means: The percentage of delivered emails that received at least one link click. (Unique clicks ÷ delivered emails) × 100.

Why it matters: CTR measures whether your email content is compelling enough to drive action. It's generally considered a more reliable engagement metric than open rate because it requires real intent.

Watch for

A low CTR on a sales email means the body copy or CTA isn't connecting, even if the subject line gets opens. A high CTR on a value email suggests strong relevance.

Improve it by

Writing more specific, benefit-focused CTAs. Reducing the number of links in the email (more options = fewer clicks on each). Making the next action feel low-risk and easy.

Benchmark context

CTR varies widely by industry and email type. Transactional and delivery emails tend to have the highest CTRs. Promotional emails tend to be lower. Compare to your own historical average rather than industry numbers.

Conversion Rate

What it means: The percentage of email recipients who completed the desired action — a purchase, a booking, a download, a signup — after clicking through from your email.

Why it matters: Conversion rate is the ultimate measure of whether your email marketing is generating business results. High open rates with low conversions suggest the email creates interest but the landing page, offer, or price isn't closing the gap.

Watch for

If clicks are high but conversions are low, the problem is usually on the landing page — not the email. Audit your sales page, checkout flow, or form if this happens.

Improve it by

Ensuring the email copy and the landing page tell a consistent story. Removing friction from the purchase or signup process. Testing different offers or price points.

Tracking it

Conversion tracking usually requires UTM parameters on your email links and a goal set up in Google Analytics or your e-commerce platform. Your email platform may not show this natively.

Unsubscribe Rate

What it means: The percentage of delivered emails that resulted in an unsubscribe. (Unsubscribes ÷ delivered emails) × 100.

Why it matters: Some unsubscribes are healthy — they clean your list of people who were never going to buy. A spike in unsubscribes after a particular email is useful feedback: something about that email's content, frequency, or targeting wasn't right.

Watch for

A sudden spike after a specific email type (e.g., a promotional email) is worth noting. Consistently high unsubscribes may mean you're emailing too frequently or not delivering on the promise that got people to sign up.

Context

An unsubscribe is not always a failure. Someone who was never going to buy leaving your list is better than them staying and marking your emails as spam.

Improve it by

Segmenting your list so people receive content relevant to them. Being honest in your lead magnet and signup process about what subscribers will receive. Not emailing more often than you have value to provide.

Bounce Rate

What it means: The percentage of emails that couldn't be delivered. Hard bounces (permanent failures — invalid address) should be removed immediately. Soft bounces (temporary failures — full inbox) are less urgent.

Why it matters: A high bounce rate signals deliverability problems. Sending to a list with many invalid addresses damages your sender reputation, which can cause your emails to land in spam for everyone — including valid subscribers.

What to do

Most email platforms automatically remove hard bounces. Check your settings to confirm this is happening. Review your list growth source — some lead sources produce higher-quality emails than others.

Prevent it by

Using a double opt-in confirmation for new subscribers. Regularly cleaning inactive addresses before they become hard bounces. Avoiding purchasing email lists.

Watch for

A sudden rise in bounce rate can indicate that your list has gone stale (not emailed in a long time) or that you've added a low-quality source. Address it quickly before it affects deliverability.

Revenue Per Email

What it means: The total revenue generated by an email campaign divided by the number of emails delivered. Shows the direct financial return of individual emails.

Why it matters: This metric connects email activity directly to business results. It's most useful during launch sequences, promotional campaigns, and sales series — where the goal is explicitly to drive purchases.

How to calculate

Total revenue from the campaign ÷ number of emails delivered. For a sequence, calculate per email and compare to see which emails in your funnel are driving the most revenue.

Improve it by

Improving your offer's relevance to the list segment you're sending to. Testing different price points. Strengthening the copy in your highest-traffic emails (announcement and close emails usually drive the most).

Long-term thinking

Value-focused emails (nurture, education, stories) often show zero direct revenue — but they increase the revenue per email on your promotional sends by building trust in the intervals between them.
