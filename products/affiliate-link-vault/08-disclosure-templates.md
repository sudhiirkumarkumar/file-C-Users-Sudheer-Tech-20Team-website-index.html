# 08 — AFFILIATE DISCLOSURE TEMPLATES

Disclosing affiliate relationships clearly is both good practice and, in many jurisdictions and on most platforms, a requirement. The templates below are starting points to customize — not legal advice.

**Important:** Disclosure rules vary by country (for example, FTC guidance in the U.S. differs from ASA rules in the UK or requirements elsewhere), by platform (Amazon Associates, YouTube, Instagram, etc. each have their own disclosure requirements), and by individual affiliate program terms. Always confirm current requirements for your specific situation with the applicable platform, network, and — where needed — a qualified professional. Nothing here should be treated as legal advice.

---

## Blog Post Disclosure

Place near the top of the post, before any affiliate links appear.

> **Template:**
> "This post contains affiliate links. If you click through and make a purchase, I may earn a commission at no additional cost to you. I only recommend products I believe are genuinely useful to [Audience Description]. Read my full disclosure policy [here/link] if you'd like more detail."

**Shorter version (inline, above the fold):**
> "Some links in this post are affiliate links — see my full disclosure [here]."

---

## YouTube Description Disclosure

Place at or near the top of the video description, and consider a verbal mention early in the video for prominent disclosure.

> **Template:**
> "This description contains affiliate links. If you use them to make a purchase, I may earn a commission, which helps support this channel at no extra cost to you."

**Verbal (spoken) version:**
> "Quick note — some of the links in the description are affiliate links, which means I may earn a small commission if you use them."

---

## Social Media Disclosure

Use platform-native disclosure tools where available (e.g., built-in "Paid partnership" / branded content labels) in addition to, not instead of, written disclosure where required.

> **Caption template:**
> "#ad #affiliate — I may earn a commission from links in this post."

**Story/Reel overlay text:**
> "Affiliate link" or "#affiliate" clearly visible on screen, not buried in hashtags at the end of a long caption.

---

## Email Marketing Disclosure

Include in any email containing affiliate links — a one-time footer disclosure across all emails, plus an inline note when a specific product is promoted, is a common approach.

> **Footer template (all emails):**
> "This email may contain affiliate links. If you purchase through them, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you."

> **Inline template (specific promotion):**
> "Heads up: the link to [Product] below is an affiliate link."

---

## Resource Page Disclosure

Place at the top of any page listing multiple recommended tools/products.

> **Template:**
> "The tools and products listed on this page include affiliate links. I may earn a commission if you make a purchase through them, at no additional cost to you. I only list tools I have used or researched and believe are relevant to [Audience Description]."

---

## Best Practices for Any Disclosure

1. **Disclose before the link, not after.** A disclosure buried at the bottom of a long page may not count as "clear and conspicuous."
2. **Use plain language.** Avoid legal jargon that an average reader wouldn't understand.
3. **Repeat disclosure across formats.** A blog disclosure doesn't cover your YouTube video or Instagram post — each platform needs its own.
4. **Check individual program requirements.** Some affiliate programs and networks require specific disclosure wording as a condition of participation — check your Program Directory (Section 03) notes for any such requirements.
5. **Review disclosures periodically.** Laws, platform policies, and program terms change — treat your disclosure wording as something to revisit, not a one-time setup task.
