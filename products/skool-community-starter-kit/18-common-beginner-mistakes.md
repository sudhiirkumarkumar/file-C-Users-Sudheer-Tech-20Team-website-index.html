# COMMON BEGINNER MISTAKES

Practical, non-judgmental breakdowns of the mistakes beginners most often make when launching a community — and what to do instead. Everyone makes some version of these; the goal is catching them early.

---

**1. Launching without clear positioning**
*Why it happens:* Excitement to "just get started" skips the harder work of defining audience and promise.
*What to do instead:* Complete the Positioning Workbook (03) before building anything else.

**2. Creating too many categories**
*Why it happens:* It feels thorough to plan for every possible topic upfront.
*What to do instead:* Start with 6-8 categories (04) and add more only when real demand shows up.

**3. Inviting everyone at once, then going quiet**
*Why it happens:* Launch day feels like the finish line instead of the starting line.
*What to do instead:* Follow the 14-Day Engagement Plan (08) — the weeks after launch matter more than launch day itself.

**4. Not replying to early posts**
*Why it happens:* It's easy to assume members will engage with each other without prompting.
*What to do instead:* Personally reply to every comment for at least the first 14 days to set the norm.

**5. Overcomplicating the classroom before launch**
*Why it happens:* Wanting everything to feel complete and polished before anyone sees it.
*What to do instead:* Launch with Modules 1-3 (05) and build the rest based on real member needs.

**6. Monetizing before building trust**
*Why it happens:* Financial pressure or excitement about the model pushes selling too early.
*What to do instead:* Use the Monetization Planner (13) to time your offer based on community trust, not just eagerness.

**7. Copying someone else's community structure exactly**
*Why it happens:* It's easier to imitate than to design from scratch.
*What to do instead:* Use the Structure Blueprint (04) as a starting example, then adjust to your specific audience.

**8. Posting inconsistently in the first month**
*Why it happens:* Momentum is highest at launch and naturally dips once the novelty fades.
*What to do instead:* Use the 30-Day Content Calendar (09) to remove the guesswork of what to post.

**9. Ignoring inactive members**
*Why it happens:* It's easier to focus on the members who are already engaged.
*What to do instead:* Use re-engagement prompts (Section 12) to reach out before members quietly disappear.

**10. Skipping rules until there's a problem**
*Why it happens:* Rules can feel unnecessary for a small, friendly early group.
*What to do instead:* Publish rules (06) at launch — they're much easier to introduce before you need to enforce them.

**11. Treating challenges as one-and-done**
*Why it happens:* Running a single challenge feels like enough structured engagement.
*What to do instead:* Rotate through the Challenge Kit (11) regularly — recurring structure builds habit and anticipation.

**12. Not tracking any metrics**
*Why it happens:* Metrics can feel like unnecessary overhead in the early days.
*What to do instead:* Use the KPI Tracker (16) from month one — even rough numbers reveal useful patterns over time.

**13. Comparing your community to large, established ones**
*Why it happens:* It's natural to benchmark against what you see publicly, which is usually the most mature stage of other communities.
*What to do instead:* Track your own growth over time using your own KPI Tracker — not someone else's current size.

**14. Making promotional posts too frequent**
*Why it happens:* Pressure to convert can lead to over-promoting, especially once monetization starts.
*What to do instead:* Follow the post-type balance guidance in the Community Post Swipe File (10) — promotional posts should be a small fraction of total content.

**15. Never auditing what's working**
*Why it happens:* Once a system is running, it's easy to assume it will keep working the same way indefinitely.
*What to do instead:* Run the Community Audit (17) monthly — communities change as they grow, and your structure should evolve with them.

**16. Assuming engagement will happen naturally**
*Why it happens:* It seems like an interesting topic should be enough to spark conversation on its own.
*What to do instead:* Use the Engagement Toolkit (12) — most participation happens because it was specifically prompted, not because it happened spontaneously.

**17. Treating the classroom and community feed as separate**
*Why it happens:* They're built as separate areas of the platform, so it's easy to manage them separately too.
*What to do instead:* Reference classroom lessons in community posts and vice versa — the two should reinforce each other.

**18. Trying to appeal to everyone**
*Why it happens:* A broader audience feels like a bigger opportunity.
*What to do instead:* Revisit your Target Audience and Member Type fields in the Positioning Workbook (03) — specificity attracts the right members faster than broad appeal does.
