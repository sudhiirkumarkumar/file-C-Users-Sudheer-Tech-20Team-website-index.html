# Section 11: PLR License & Compliance Guide

**Important:** This section is educational only. It does not grant, interpret, or override any specific PLR license. There is no such thing as a universal PLR license — every provider sets their own terms, and those terms can vary dramatically even for products that look similar. Always read the actual license document that came with the specific PLR you purchased before you sell, edit, or distribute anything built from it.

---

## Why the License Matters More Than the Content

Two PLR packs can look nearly identical and still have completely different rules attached. One provider might allow full resale rights and rebranding; another might restrict you to using the content for your own personal blog only, with no resale allowed at all. Skipping this step is the most common way beginners accidentally violate a license they never actually read.

---

## What to Check Before You Sell Anything

### 1. Read Your Specific PLR License
Locate the exact license document (not a general FAQ page) that came with your purchase. Save a copy for your own records.

### 2. Check Modification Rights
- Are you allowed to edit, rewrite, shorten, or expand the content?
- Are there any restrictions on how much you must change before reselling?

### 3. Check Resale Rights
- Are you allowed to sell the content (edited or as-is) to your own customers?
- Are there restrictions on price, platform, or format?

### 4. Check Giveaway Rights
- Are you allowed to give the product away for free (e.g., as a lead magnet or bonus)?
- Some licenses that allow resale do not allow giveaways, and vice versa.

### 5. Check Whether Source Files Can Be Redistributed
- Are you allowed to hand over the original editable source files (e.g., Word, Canva) to your customers, or only a finished, non-editable version (like a locked PDF)?

### 6. Check Whether Editable Templates Can Be Resold
- If you're offering an editable Canva template as part of your product, confirm your license allows redistributing an editable version — this is treated differently from a static/finished file by many providers.

### 7. Check Whether PLR Branding Must Be Removed
- Some licenses require you to remove all mention of the original PLR provider, source website, or watermark before distributing your version.

### 8. Keep Proof of Purchase / License Terms
- Save your receipt, license document, and the date of purchase.
- Providers occasionally update their terms — keep the version that was in effect when you bought the product.

---

## "Before You Sell" License Checklist

- [ ] I have located and read the specific license document for this PLR product
- [ ] I know whether I'm allowed to modify the content
- [ ] I know whether I'm allowed to resell it (and any conditions attached)
- [ ] I know whether I'm allowed to give it away for free
- [ ] I know whether I can distribute source/editable files or only a finished version
- [ ] I know whether editable templates specifically can be resold
- [ ] I have removed any required PLR provider branding or attribution
- [ ] I have saved my proof of purchase and license terms
- [ ] I am not claiming copyright ownership beyond what my license grants
- [ ] I am not representing the content as more original than my transformation level actually supports (see Section 4)

---

## A Note on Claims

Never claim rights that your license doesn't explicitly grant. If you're unsure whether something is allowed, the safest options are:

1. Contact the PLR provider directly and ask.
2. Choose the more conservative interpretation until you get clarity.
3. When in doubt, apply Level 3 transformation (Section 4) — the more original your final product is, the less your legal exposure depends on interpreting someone else's license terms.

This kit provides a repeatable *process* for rebranding PLR responsibly. It is not legal advice, and license terms always take precedence over any general guidance in this guide.
