# Section 7: Cover & Branding Guide

A beginner-friendly branding system so your rebranded product looks professionally designed — even with no design background.

---

## Brand Color Selection

- Choose **one primary color** (used most — headers, cover background, icons)
- Choose **one secondary color** (accents, buttons, highlights)
- Choose **one neutral** (body text — almost always a dark charcoal or near-black, not pure black)
- Choose **one background** (usually white or a very light neutral)

**Beginner rule:** limit yourself to 3–4 colors total. More than that starts to look cluttered and inconsistent.

---

## Font Pairing

Pick **one heading font** and **one body font** — never more than two font families in a single product.

| Pairing style | Heading Font Style | Body Font Style |
|---|---|---|
| Clean & modern | Bold sans-serif | Simple sans-serif |
| Classic & professional | Serif | Clean sans-serif |
| Soft & approachable | Rounded sans-serif | Light sans-serif |
| Editorial | Elegant serif | Simple serif or sans-serif |

**Beginner rule:** if in doubt, pair a bold sans-serif heading with a simple, highly legible sans-serif body font. It's the safest combination for readability across every device.

---

## Cover Hierarchy

Every cover should have a clear order the eye follows:

1. **Title** (largest, boldest element)
2. **Subtitle** (smaller, supports the title)
3. **Visual element** (icon, simple graphic, or photo — optional, not required)
4. **Brand name / author name** (smallest, usually bottom of the cover)

Avoid competing focal points — if everything is bold and large, nothing stands out.

---

## Typography Rules

- Headings: consistent size and weight across every section divider
- Body text: minimum 11–12pt for print, 16px for digital screens
- Line spacing: 1.4–1.6x for body text so it doesn't feel cramped
- Avoid using more than 2 font weights (e.g., regular and bold) throughout the document
- Never use all-caps for long stretches of body text — reserve it for short labels or headers

---

## Page Layout

- Keep consistent margins on every page (minimum 0.5"–0.75")
- Align text to a consistent grid — don't mix centered and left-aligned text randomly
- Repeat the same header/footer style across every page for consistency
- Number every page except the cover

---

## White Space

White space is not wasted space — it's what makes a page feel premium instead of cramped.

- Leave breathing room around headings (extra space above, slightly less below)
- Don't fill every inch of a page with text or graphics
- Use white space to visually separate sections instead of heavy borders or boxes

---

## Icon Usage

- Use one consistent icon style throughout (all outline icons, or all filled icons — never mixed)
- Use icons to support checklists, section headers, or callouts — not as decoration
- Keep icon colors limited to your brand palette

---

## Image Selection

- Choose images with consistent lighting and color tone throughout the product
- Avoid generic, obviously-stocky images that don't match your niche
- If using photos of people, make sure they reflect your actual target audience
- When in doubt, use simple illustrations, icons, or abstract shapes instead of photos — they're easier to keep consistent and less likely to look "stock"

---

## CTA (Call-to-Action) Placement

- Place a clear CTA at the end of the introduction (pointing to how to use the product)
- Place a CTA on the final page (pointing to your shop, website, or next product)
- Keep CTA buttons/boxes visually distinct using your secondary brand color
- Never use more than one CTA per page — competing CTAs reduce action on both

---

## 5 Ready-Made Visual Style Directions

Pick one direction and apply it consistently across your entire product.

### 1. Minimal Professional
- **Color direction:** Black, white, and one muted accent (navy, forest green, or terracotta)
- **Font style:** Clean modern sans-serif for both headings and body
- **Image style:** Simple line icons, little to no photography
- **Layout style:** Generous white space, left-aligned text, thin dividing lines
- **Suitable niches:** Business, productivity, career, finance education

### 2. Modern Creator
- **Color direction:** Bright but limited palette (e.g., coral + deep teal + cream)
- **Font style:** Bold rounded sans-serif headings, simple sans-serif body
- **Image style:** Flat illustrations, bold geometric shapes
- **Layout style:** Asymmetric section headers, colorful callout boxes
- **Suitable niches:** Social media, content creation, freelancing

### 3. Soft Premium
- **Color direction:** Muted pastels with a warm neutral base (blush, sage, cream, soft gold accent)
- **Font style:** Light serif headings, simple sans-serif body
- **Image style:** Soft-toned photography or delicate line illustrations
- **Layout style:** Airy spacing, rounded corners, subtle dividers
- **Suitable niches:** Personal development, coaching, wellness, journaling

### 4. Bold Business
- **Color direction:** High contrast — dark background sections paired with a bright accent (e.g., charcoal + electric blue or bright yellow)
- **Font style:** Heavy sans-serif headings, clean sans-serif body
- **Image style:** Strong geometric graphics, minimal photography
- **Layout style:** Structured grids, bold section dividers, confident use of color blocks
- **Suitable niches:** Marketing, small business, consulting

### 5. Clean Editorial
- **Color direction:** Black, white, and a single deep accent color (burgundy, navy, or forest)
- **Font style:** Classic serif headings, simple sans-serif body
- **Image style:** High-quality, editorial-style photography or none at all
- **Layout style:** Magazine-style layouts, pull quotes, clear typographic hierarchy
- **Suitable niches:** Coaching, personal development, finance education, career
