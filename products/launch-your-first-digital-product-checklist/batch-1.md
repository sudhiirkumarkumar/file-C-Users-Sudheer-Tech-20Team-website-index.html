## SECTION 1 — QUICK START

### How to use this checklist
This is not a book to read once and put away. It is a working tool.

1. Go section by section, in order — each one builds on the last.
2. Check off ☐ boxes the moment you finish a step. Momentum matters more than speed.
3. Fill in every blank field (product name, price, dates) as soon as you know the answer — don't leave it for "later."
4. Keep this file open next to you while you build. Treat it like a project manager, not a novel.

### What you need before starting
☐ A device to create your product (laptop, tablet, or phone)
☐ A free tool to build the product (Canva, Google Docs, Google Sheets, or Notion)
☐ 3–7 days you can realistically dedicate in short daily blocks (30–90 minutes)
☐ One idea, skill, or piece of knowledge you can turn into something useful
☐ A bank account or payment method to receive money (PayPal, Stripe, or your bank)

You do **not** need: a website, an audience, a logo, business registration, or design skills. All of that can come later.

### Recommended completion path
- **Beginner path (recommended):** Complete every section in order, section 1 → 17.
- **Fast path (if you already have an idea):** Skip to Section 3 (Validation), then continue in order.
- **Relaunch path (you already sold something):** Start at Section 15 (After the Launch).

### Launch readiness overview
| Stage | Sections | Goal |
|---|---|---|

### "Don't try to make it perfect" reminder
> Your first version only needs to be **useful, clear, and honest.** Perfect is a version 2 problem. A finished simple product earns money. An unfinished perfect product earns nothing.

## SECTION 3 — IDEA VALIDATION

**Goal:** Confirm real people already want this before you spend time building it.

### Action Steps
☐ **Identify competitors.** Search your product idea + "template," "checklist," "guide," or "toolkit" on Etsy, Gumroad, Google, and Pinterest. List 3–5.

☐ **Search marketplaces** (Etsy, Gumroad, Payhip, Creative Market, Notion template galleries) for similar products.

☐ **Check customer reviews** on those competing products — read the 3-star and 4-star reviews specifically; they reveal what's missing.

☐ **Identify repeated customer problems** mentioned across multiple reviews or comments.

☐ **Search social media discussions** (Facebook groups, Reddit, TikTok comments, Instagram comments) for people asking about this problem.

☐ **Look for existing demand** — are people actively asking "does anyone have a template for..." or "how do I..."?

☐ **Identify gaps in competing products** — outdated, incomplete, overpriced, poorly designed, or missing beginner explanations.

☐ **Define your unique angle** — simpler, more beginner-friendly, more complete, better designed, more affordable, or more specific niche.

☐ **Validate the product promise** — does your one-sentence promise (Section 2) address a gap you just found? Adjust it if needed.

☐ **Decide whether to proceed, modify, or reject the idea.**

## SECTION 5 — CREATE THE PRODUCT

**Goal:** Produce a finished, usable first version.

### Action Steps (in order)
☐ Create the product outline — list every section/page before writing content.
☐ Create the first draft — write/build without editing as you go.
☐ Organize the content — logical order, numbered steps, clear headings.
☐ Add practical examples — real scenarios, not just theory.
☐ Add worksheets/checklists/templates where appropriate — these boost perceived value the most.
☐ Create an attractive design — consistent fonts, colors, and spacing (Canva is enough).
☐ Check formatting — headings, spacing, alignment consistent throughout.
☐ Check spelling and grammar — read it aloud or use a free grammar checker.
☐ Test every link — every URL must open correctly.
☐ Test downloadable files — open each file on a different device than the one you built it on.
☐ Review the product from the customer's perspective — would a stranger understand it with zero help from you?
☐ Make final improvements based on that review.
☐ Export the final version — correct file format (usually PDF for guides, .xlsx/.csv for sheets, .pptx or Canva link for templates).

### FINAL PRODUCT QUALITY CHECK
Rate each 1–5, or simply check when true:
☐ Useful — solves the stated problem
☐ Easy to understand — no confusing jargon
☐ Easy to use — customer knows what to do without asking questions
☐ Visually professional — consistent, clean design
☐ Error-free — spelling, grammar, broken links all checked
☐ Action-oriented — tells the customer what to *do*, not just what to *know*
☐ Worth the price — you would pay for this yourself

## SECTION 6 — PACKAGE YOUR DIGITAL PRODUCT

**Goal:** Turn your finished file(s) into a clean, professional delivery package.

### Action Steps
☐ Main product file
☐ Bonus files (if any)
☐ Instructions/read-me file — one page explaining how to use everything
☐ License/usage terms — state clearly if it's for personal use only or resale is allowed
☐ Thank-you page — a short "thank you for buying" file or page
☐ Support/contact information — an email address customers can reach
☐ File naming — clear, numbered, no vague names like "final_v3_new.pdf"
☐ Folder organization — one main folder, clearly labeled
☐ ZIP package — if delivering more than one file, zip it into a single download
