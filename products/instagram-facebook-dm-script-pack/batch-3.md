## Section 11: Price & Value Objections

### Script 101 — Direct "Too Expensive" Pushback
**Situation:**
A lead flatly says the price is too high, and you want to reframe around value rather than just defending the number.
**DM:**
I hear you, [Name] — can I share why it's priced this way? [Product/Service] includes [Key Value Point], which is usually what makes the difference for people. Happy to break it down further.
**Personalize:**
[Name], [Product/Service], [Key Value Point]
**Tip:**
Explain the "why" behind the price once, then stop — repeating it can sound defensive.

### Script 102 — Comparing Price to a Cheaper Competitor
**Situation:**
A lead mentions a competitor offers something similar for less.
**DM:**
Totally fair to compare, [Name]. The main difference with [Product/Service] is [Differentiator] — that's usually why customers choose it even at a higher price. Want me to walk through the comparison?
**Personalize:**
[Name], [Product/Service], [Differentiator]
**Tip:**
Never disparage the competitor — focus entirely on what makes your offer different.

### Script 103 — "I Can Find This Cheaper Elsewhere"
**Situation:**
A lead states outright they've seen it cheaper somewhere else.
**DM:**
That's good to know, [Name] — mind sharing where? Sometimes the lower price means a few things are left out, and I want to make sure you're comparing apples to apples.
**Personalize:**
[Name]
**Tip:**
Asking "where" is genuinely useful — it lets you address the specific gap rather than guessing.

### Script 104 — "Why Is It Priced This Way?"
**Situation:**
A lead is curious (not hostile) about how you arrived at your pricing.
**DM:**
Good question, [Name]! The price covers [Cost Factor 1] and [Cost Factor 2], which is what allows me to deliver [Outcome] consistently. Happy to go deeper if useful.
**Personalize:**
[Name], [Cost Factor 1], [Cost Factor 2], [Outcome]
**Tip:**
Transparency about pricing structure often removes the objection entirely.

### Script 105 — Asking for a Discount Directly
**Situation:**
A lead asks outright if you can lower the price.
**DM:**
I appreciate you asking directly, [Name]. I keep pricing consistent for everyone so it stays fair, but I can offer [Alternative — e.g., payment plan, smaller package] if that helps.
**Personalize:**
[Name], [Alternative]
**Tip:**
Offering a structural alternative (not a random discount) protects your pricing integrity.

### Script 106 — Justifying Premium Pricing
**Situation:**
A lead questions why your offer costs more than typical options in the market.
**DM:**
Fair question, [Name] — [Product/Service] is priced at the premium end because of [Specific Reason, e.g., level of support, quality, results]. It's not for everyone, but it's built for people who want [Outcome].
**Personalize:**
[Name], [Product/Service], [Specific Reason], [Outcome]
**Tip:**
Owning the premium positioning confidently reads better than apologizing for the price.

### Script 107 — Price Shock After Seeing Full Package
**Situation:**
A lead reacts with surprise or hesitation after seeing the full price of a package or bundle.
**DM:**
I get that it's a bigger number at first glance, [Name] — want me to show how it breaks down per [Unit, e.g., month/session]? Sometimes it reads differently that way.
**Personalize:**
[Name], [Unit]
**Tip:**
Breaking a large total into smaller units often reframes the perceived cost.

### Script 108 — Payment Plan/Installment Objection
**Situation:**
A lead can't pay the full amount upfront and hesitates because of it.
**DM:**
No problem at all, [Name] — I do offer a payment plan option: [Plan Details]. Would that make it easier to move forward?
**Personalize:**
[Name], [Plan Details]
**Tip:**
Mention payment plans proactively if you have one — many leads won't ask unless prompted.

### Script 109 — "I Don't See the Value"
**Situation:**
A lead isn't convinced the price matches what they'd get in return.
**DM:**
Appreciate you being upfront, [Name]. Can you tell me what outcome would make this feel worth it to you? That'll help me show whether [Product/Service] actually delivers that.
**Personalize:**
[Name], [Product/Service]
**Tip:**
Let them define "value" in their own words rather than assuming what will convince them.

### Script 110 — Objection After a Price Increase
**Situation:**
An existing or returning customer questions a recent price increase.
**DM:**
Totally understand the question, [Name] — the price changed because of [Reason, e.g., added features, increased demand]. I wanted to make sure the value kept pace with it. Happy to talk through what's new.
**Personalize:**
[Name], [Reason]
**Tip:**
Pair every price increase explanation with what improved — it reframes the increase as added value.

**Personalize:**
[Name], [Alternative]
**Tip:**
Pairing "no" with an alternative keeps the conversation constructive instead of feeling like a dead end.

State the policy factually, without apologizing excessively — over-apologizing can undermine your position.
