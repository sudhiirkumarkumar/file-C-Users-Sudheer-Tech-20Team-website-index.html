## Welcome

Most small businesses don't fail for lack of effort. They struggle because the owner is working hard on an unclear strategy: selling to "everyone", competing only on price, describing the business differently every time, and planning week to week.

This workbook fixes that with a structured process. You will not write a 40-page business plan. You will make a short sequence of clear decisions — each one recorded on a worksheet, each one supported by an AI prompt that does the heavy drafting for you — and finish with:

- **A One-Page Strategy Summary** you can hand to a team member, a partner, or your AI assistant.
- **A defined customer**, backed by real evidence rather than guesswork.
- **A clear offer, USP and price** that you can explain in one sentence.
- **A 90-day roadmap** with weekly priorities and a risk plan.

Everything you produce here becomes the input for the rest of the AI Business Operating System: your marketing (System 02), sales scripts (System 03), Claude Business Assistant (System 08) and 30-Day Implementation Plan (System 09) all draw on the decisions you make in this workbook.

> **A note on AI.** AI is a fast, tireless drafting partner. It is not a source of truth about your market, your customers or the law. Every prompt in this workbook is designed to work on information *you* provide, and every output must be reviewed by you before you act on it.

### AI working rules (read once, follow always)

1. **Review before you act.** Treat every AI output as a first draft. You are the decision-maker.
2. **AI doesn't know your market.** It cannot reliably tell you local prices, competitor details, demand levels or current regulations. Where a prompt needs current facts, it marks them **[VERIFY CURRENT INFORMATION]** — check those yourself.
3. **No invented numbers.** If an output contains a statistic, a market size or a percentage you did not provide, delete it or verify it from a reliable source.
4. **Protect sensitive information.** Do not paste customer phone numbers, personal addresses, bank details, passwords, ID numbers or confidential contracts. Use initials or codes (e.g. "Customer C-014").
5. **Legal, tax and financial topics.** Outputs here are general and educational. For GST, contracts, licences, employment or financial decisions, confirm with a qualified professional.
6. **Keep your voice.** Edit AI drafts so they sound like you. Customers buy from people they trust.

**Time:** 20–30 minutes.

# LAYER 1 — FOUNDATION

*How does this business make money, and is the idea sound?*

## Tool 1.1 — Business Model Map

**Use this when:** starting a new business, adding a new service line, or when the business "works" but you can't explain why profits are thin.
**Time:** 40–60 minutes.
**You will produce:** W-STR-01 — a one-page, nine-block map of how your business creates, delivers and captures value.

### How it works

The Business Model Map is Aqvani's small-business adaptation of the widely used one-page business model idea. It is ordered the way a small-business owner thinks: **customer first, money last**. Each block depends on the one above it.

| # | Block | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer | Who specifically pays you? (Who uses it, if different?) |
| 2 | Problem | What problem or desire makes them look for a solution? |
| 3 | Offer | What exactly do you sell, and what result does it create? |
| 4 | Channels | How do they discover you, buy from you, and receive what they bought? |
| 5 | Relationship | How do you keep them — one-off, repeat, subscription, community? |
| 6 | Revenue | How and when do you get paid? Price, model, frequency. |
| 7 | Key activities | What must you do well every week for this to work? |
| 8 | Key resources & partners | What people, tools, assets, suppliers or partners do you depend on? |
| 9 | Costs | What does it cost to deliver — fixed and variable? |

Then two connecting questions turn the map into a diagnosis:

- **The profit line:** Does block 6 comfortably exceed block 9 per customer, after your time is counted?
- **The weak link:** Which single block, if it failed, would break the business?

### Steps

1. Fill blocks 1–3 first. If you can't, go to Layer 2 and come back.
2. Fill blocks 4–9 using today's reality, not plans.
3. For each block, add one note: ✔ strong, ~ unclear, ✖ weak.
4. Run **P-STR-002** to tidy and complete the map, then **P-STR-003** to stress-test it.
5. Write your weak link and one action to strengthen it at the bottom of W-STR-01.

### Common mistakes

- **Describing the ideal customer instead of the paying customer.** Parents may pay for a child's coaching; the child uses it. Write both.
- **Leaving your own time out of costs.** If you work 50 hours a week, that time has a cost even if you don't pay yourself a salary.
- **Treating "social media" as a channel.** Be specific: "Instagram Reels → DM → WhatsApp → UPI payment".

## Tool 1.2 — Business Idea Validator

**Use this when:** you are considering a new business, product, service, market or price point — before you spend significant money or time on it.
**Time:** 30 minutes to score; 1–14 days to run a validation test.
**You will produce:** W-STR-02 — an evidence-based idea score out of 40 and one low-cost experiment to run next.

### How it works

Most ideas are judged on enthusiasm. The Idea Validator judges them on eight criteria, each scored 1–5, and — critically — **each score needs evidence**. A score without evidence is capped at 2.

| # | Criterion | 1 = weak | 5 = strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem urgency | Nice-to-have | Painful, frequent, costly to ignore |
| 2 | Customer clarity | "Anyone could use it" | You can name 10 real people who fit |
| 3 | Willingness to pay | No one pays for this today | People already pay for poor solutions |
| 4 | Reachability | You don't know where they gather | You already reach them cheaply |
| 5 | Differentiation | Identical to many options | Clear, provable difference |
| 6 | Delivery capability | You'd need new skills, staff or capital | You can deliver it well today |
| 7 | Economics | Margin unclear or thin | Healthy margin after your time |
| 8 | Owner fit | Drains you or clashes with your life | Fits your skills, time and interests |

**Evidence levels** (write one beside each score):
- **E0 — Opinion.** "I think…" → maximum score 2.
- **E1 — Indirect.** Competitors exist, people ask online, reviews mention the problem → maximum 4.
- **E2 — Direct.** Real conversations, pre-orders, deposits, paid pilots → up to 5.

**Verdict bands:**

| Total | Verdict | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| 32–40 | **Build** | Proceed; validate pricing with real sales |
| 24–31 | **Test** | Run a low-cost experiment on the weakest criteria |
| 16–23 | **Rework** | Change the customer, problem or offer, then re-score |
| 8–15 | **Park** | Record it and return if circumstances change |

### Steps

1. Write the idea in one sentence: "[OFFER] for [CUSTOMER] who want [OUTCOME], delivered via [FORMAT] at about [PRICE]."
2. Score each criterion and record the evidence level.
3. Total the score and read the verdict.
4. Run **P-STR-004** to challenge your scoring, then **P-STR-005** to design a test for your two weakest criteria.
5. Run the test. Re-score with the new evidence.

# LAYER 2 — CUSTOMER

*Who exactly do I serve, and what problem do they pay to solve?*

> **Why this layer matters most.** Almost every later decision — your USP, your marketing message, your price, your content — depends on how precisely you understand your customer. If you only have time for one layer this week, do this one.

## Tool 2.1 — Niche Research Framework

**Use this when:** you serve "everyone", you are starting out, or you are thinking about specialising.
**Time:** 45–60 minutes.
**You will produce:** W-STR-03 — a scored comparison of 3–6 candidate niches and one chosen focus niche.

### How it works

A niche is a specific group of customers with a shared situation and a shared problem. "Small businesses" is a market. "Independent cafés in Bengaluru that want more weekday footfall" is a niche.

Choosing a niche does not mean refusing other customers. It means **your marketing speaks to one group clearly**, so that group recognises itself immediately.

The Aqvani **Niche Filter** scores each candidate niche on six criteria (1–5 each, total /30):

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| **Pain** | Do they have an urgent, recurring problem you can solve? |
| **Pay** | Do they have the budget and habit of paying for solutions? |
| **Reach** | Can you find and reach them affordably (groups, places, platforms, associations)? |
| **Edge** | Do you have experience, results, access or understanding others don't? |
| **Size** | Are there enough of them to meet your goal — without being so big you get lost? |
| **Energy** | Will you enjoy working with them for the next 2–3 years? |

### Steps

1. List 3–6 candidate niches. Use this pattern: **[WHO] + [SITUATION] + [PROBLEM]**.
2. Generate more candidates with **P-STR-006** if you're stuck.
3. Score each on the six criteria. Mark scores you're unsure of with "?".
4. Run **P-STR-007** to challenge your scores and recommend a focus.
5. Choose one focus niche. Write why, and what would make you change your mind.

## Tool 2.3 — Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

**Use this when:** you need to decide which leads to pursue and which to politely decline — especially for B2B services, consulting, freelancing or high-value orders.
**Time:** 30–40 minutes.
**You will produce:** W-STR-05 — an ICP with weighted scoring criteria and red flags. Use the **ICP Scorecard** sheet in the Excel file to score real prospects.

### How it works

The persona describes a *person* so you can communicate with them. The ICP describes the *best-fit customer account* so you can **qualify** them. Think of it as a filter for your time.

An ICP has three parts:

1. **Fit criteria** — the characteristics of your best customers (e.g. business size, budget, urgency, location, decision-maker access).
2. **Weights** — how much each criterion matters (totalling 100%).
3. **Red flags** — signs that a customer is likely to be unprofitable or difficult, regardless of score.

**Scoring:** each criterion is rated 0–5 for a prospect. Fit % = sum of (rating × weight) ÷ 5.

| Fit % | Band | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 80–100% | **Ideal** | Prioritise; respond same day |
| 60–79% | **Good** | Pursue with standard process |
| 40–59% | **Possible** | Offer a lighter or self-serve option |
| Below 40% | **Poor** | Decline politely or refer elsewhere |

A single red flag can override a good score.

### Steps

1. List your 5 best and 3 worst customers (anonymised). What do the best have in common? What did the worst share?
2. Turn the patterns into 5–7 criteria.
3. Assign weights that total 100%.
4. Write 3–5 red flags.
5. Run **P-STR-010** to refine. Enter the criteria and weights into the ICP Scorecard sheet.
6. Score your last 5 leads to test whether the scores match your gut feel.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | | | |
| 2 | | | | |
| 3 | | | | |
| 4 | | | | |
| 5 | | | | |
| | | | **Total** | **100%** |

## Tool 2.4 — Customer Pain Point Finder

**Use this when:** you need to know which customer problems to lead with in your offer, content and sales messages.
**Time:** 45 minutes.
**You will produce:** W-STR-06 — a ranked list of pain points scored by evidence. Use the **Pain Point Scoring** sheet in the Excel file for automatic scoring.

### How it works

Customers mention many problems. Only some of them are worth building your offer around. The Pain Point Finder scores each pain on four factors (1–5):

| Factor | Question | Default weight |
|---|---|---|
| **Frequency** | How often does this pain occur or come up in conversations? | 20% |
| **Intensity** | How much does it hurt (cost, stress, risk, embarrassment)? | 30% |
| **Willingness to pay** | Do people spend money or effort to fix it today? | 30% |
| **Ability to solve** | Can you solve it well with your current capability? | 20% |

**Pain Score** = weighted average of the four ratings × 20 → a score from 20 to 100.

| Score | Priority | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| 75–100 | **High** | Lead with this in your offer, headline and sales conversations |
| 55–74 | **Medium** | Use in content, FAQs and supporting messages |
| Below 55 | **Low** | Note it; don't build around it |

**Where to find pains (anonymise everything):** your WhatsApp and email enquiries · questions asked on sales calls · your reviews · competitors' 1–3 star reviews · comments on competitors' posts · online communities where your customers ask for help.

### Steps

1. Collect at least 15 pieces of customer language from the sources above.
2. Run **P-STR-011** to extract and group the pain points.
3. Enter each pain into W-STR-06 or the Excel sheet. Score the four factors.
4. Run **P-STR-012** to challenge your scores and connect top pains to your offer.
5. Mark your top 3 pains. These feed directly into Layer 4 and System 02.

## Tool 2.5 — Problem–Solution Fit

**Use this when:** you have a top pain and an offer, and need to confirm the offer genuinely solves that pain better than what customers do today.
**Time:** 30 minutes.
**You will produce:** W-STR-07 — a fit verdict with the gaps you need to close.

### How it works

Problem–solution fit means: **a specific customer has a specific problem, and your solution addresses it in a way they prefer over their current alternative.** The Aqvani Fit Test asks six questions:

| # | Fit test | Yes / Partly / No |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Direct** — Does the solution address the top pain directly, not indirectly? | |
| 2 | **Better** — Is it clearly better than the current alternative on something the customer cares about? | |
| 3 | **Visible** — Can the customer see or feel the result quickly? | |
| 4 | **Easy** — Is it easy to start (low effort, low risk, low confusion)? | |
| 5 | **Affordable** — Is the price reasonable relative to the cost of the problem? | |
| 6 | **Proven** — Do you have any evidence it works (past results, pilot, demonstration)? | |

**Verdict:** 5–6 Yes → **Strong fit** · 3–4 Yes → **Partial fit — fix the gaps** · 0–2 Yes → **Weak fit — rethink the solution**. (Count "Partly" as half.)

### Steps

1. Write the problem statement: "[CUSTOMER] struggles with [PAIN] because [ROOT CAUSE], which costs them [CONSEQUENCE]."
2. List current alternatives (including "do nothing" and "do it themselves").
3. Describe your solution in two sentences.
4. Answer the six fit tests honestly.
5. Run **P-STR-013** for an objective review and a gap-closing plan.

## Tool 3.2 — Competitor Comparison Worksheet

**Use this when:** you have competitor profiles and need to see, side by side, where you are stronger, weaker and different.
**Time:** 30–45 minutes.
**You will produce:** W-STR-09 — a scored comparison grid and a gap summary.

### How it works

- **Gaps:** factors customers care about where every competitor scores low. These are opportunities.

| 4 | Reliability / consistency | 9 | After-sales support |
| 5 | Customer communication | 10 | Brand presence / visibility |

Add a **"Customer importance"** column (1–5) so a gap on a high-importance factor stands out.

## Tool 3.3 — SWOT Framework

**Use this when:** you need a clear, honest picture of your position before setting direction — and want to convert it into actions, not just a list.
**Time:** 40 minutes.
**You will produce:** W-STR-10 — a SWOT grid plus the Aqvani **SWOT Action Converter**: four sets of strategic moves.

### How it works

- **Strengths** and **Weaknesses** are *internal* — things you control (skills, team, systems, reputation, cash, assets).
- **Opportunities** and **Threats** are *external* — things you don't control (customer trends, competitor moves, platform changes, seasons, regulations, costs).

A SWOT is only useful if it leads to decisions. The Action Converter pairs the quadrants:

| | **Opportunities** | **Threats** |
|---|---|---|
| **Strengths** | **GROW moves** — use strengths to capture opportunities | **DEFEND moves** — use strengths to reduce threats |
| **Weaknesses** | **FIX moves** — fix weaknesses that block opportunities | **PROTECT moves** — reduce exposure where weak and threatened |

**Rules for a useful SWOT:**
1. Maximum 5 items per quadrant — the most important ones.
2. Every item must be specific. "Good quality" → "Every piece hand-finished and quality-checked before dispatch".
3. Every item needs evidence or a clear reason.
4. Opportunities and Threats must be external. "Launch a new product" is an action, not an opportunity.

### Steps

1. Fill the four quadrants using your Layer 1–3 outputs.
2. Run **P-STR-018** to sharpen and verify the items.
3. Run **P-STR-019** to generate GROW, DEFEND, FIX and PROTECT moves.
4. Choose a maximum of 3 moves to carry into your 90-day plan (Layer 5).

# LAYER 4 — OFFER

## Tool 4.1 — USP Builder

**Use this when:** you can't answer "Why should I choose you over [alternative]?" in one convincing sentence.
**Time:** 40 minutes.
**You will produce:** W-STR-11 — a Unique Selling Proposition that is relevant, different, provable and sustainable.

### How it works

A USP is not a slogan. It is the specific reason your best-fit customer should choose you over their alternatives. The Aqvani **RDPS Filter** tests every candidate differentiator:

| Test | Question | Fails if… |
|---|---|---|
| **R — Relevant** | Does it address one of my top 3 pains? | Customers don't care about it |
| **D — Different** | Do competitors fail to offer or claim it? | Everyone says the same |
| **P — Provable** | Can I show evidence (process, record, demonstration, guarantee terms, examples)? | It's just a claim |
| **S — Sustainable** | Can I keep delivering it as I grow, and is it hard to copy quickly? | It depends on a temporary situation |

**USP formula:**
> **We [specific approach or result] for [specific customer], so they [benefit linked to top pain] — [proof].**

### Steps

1. List 8–12 things that are genuinely different about your business: process, speciality, experience, location, speed, materials, support, terms, team, story.
2. Run each through the RDPS filter (✔ / ✖ for each test).
3. Keep the 1–3 differentiators that pass all four tests.
4. Draft your USP with **P-STR-020**, then pressure-test it with **P-STR-021**.
5. Write down the proof you will show customers.

## Tool 4.2 — Offer Builder

**Use this when:** you need to define exactly what a customer receives, so it is easy to understand, buy, deliver and price.
**Time:** 45–60 minutes. (For the full step-by-step build — scope, naming, tiers and a sales-ready one-pager — use the separate **Offer Builder** file.)
**You will produce:** W-STR-12 — a complete offer definition.

### How it works

Customers don't buy services or products; they buy **a clear outcome, delivered in a clear way, under clear terms**. The Aqvani **Offer Stack** has eight components:

| # | Component | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Outcome** | What result or change does the customer get? |
| 2 | **Core deliverables** | What exactly do they receive? (Countable items.) |
| 3 | **Format & delivery** | How is it delivered? Where? By whom? |
| 4 | **Timeline** | How long until they get the result? What happens when? |
| 5 | **Support** | What help do they get during and after? |
| 6 | **Bonuses / extras** | What additional items increase value without large extra cost? |
| 7 | **Terms** | Payment, scheduling, cancellation, refunds, what's not included |
| 8 | **Price** | What does it cost, and how is it paid? (Detailed in Tool 4.5.) |

A strong offer passes the **"Explain it in 30 seconds" test**: a friend could repeat it accurately after hearing it once.

### Steps

1. Start from your top pain and USP. Write the outcome first.
2. Fill the eight components on W-STR-12.
3. Run **P-STR-022** to sharpen the offer.
4. If you want options for different budgets, run **P-STR-023** for tiered versions.
5. Read the offer aloud to someone outside the business. Fix anything they misunderstand.

## Tool 4.3 — Value Proposition Builder

**Use this when:** you need the main message for your website, profile, brochure or sales page — the words that connect what customers want with what you provide.
**Time:** 30–40 minutes.
**You will produce:** W-STR-13 — the Aqvani **Value Bridge**, plus a headline, sub-headline and three proof points.

### How it works

The Value Bridge has two sides and a connecting message:

| CUSTOMER SIDE | ↔ | YOUR SIDE |
|---|---|---|
| **Situation** — what they're dealing with | | **What you provide** — your offer components |
| **Pains** — what frustrates, worries or costs them | | **Pain relievers** — how each pain is reduced |
| **Desired outcomes** — what they want to have, feel or achieve | | **Outcome creators** — how you help them get there |

Every item on the right must connect to something on the left. Anything on the right that connects to nothing is a feature customers don't care about — remove it from your main message.

**The bridge message:**
- **Headline** — the outcome, in customer words (max 10 words).
- **Sub-headline** — who it's for, what you do, and how (max 25 words).
- **Three proof points** — specific, verifiable reasons to believe.

### Steps

1. Fill the customer side from your persona and pain points. Use customer language.
2. Fill your side from your offer and USP.
3. Draw a line (or write the number) from each item on your side to the item it serves.
4. Run **P-STR-024** to draft the bridge message.
5. Test the headline: would your persona stop scrolling for it?

## Tool 4.4 — Product Positioning Framework

**Use this when:** customers compare you with alternatives and you need to define, deliberately, where you sit in their mind.
**Time:** 30 minutes.
**You will produce:** W-STR-14 — a positioning statement and a positioning map.

### How it works

Positioning answers: **"Compared to what, and why you?"** It is internal — a decision that guides all your messaging — rather than a customer-facing slogan.

**Aqvani positioning statement:**
> **[BRAND] helps [SPECIFIC AUDIENCE] [ACHIEVE OUTCOME] through [CATEGORY / APPROACH]. Unlike [MAIN ALTERNATIVE], we [KEY DIFFERENCE] — proven by [REASON TO BELIEVE].**

**Positioning map:** choose two dimensions customers care about (from your comparison grid) and plot yourself and your competitors. Common pairs:

| Dimension pair | Useful for |
|---|---|
| Price ↔ Level of personal service | Services, consulting |
| Specialist ↔ Generalist | Freelancers, agencies |
| Do-it-yourself ↔ Done-for-you | Digital products, training |
| Speed ↔ Depth / thoroughness | Repairs, professional services |
| Mass-produced ↔ Handmade / unique | Products, e-commerce |

You want an empty or uncrowded area on the map that customers value.

### Steps

1. Pick your **category** — the familiar box customers will put you in (e.g. "operations consultant", "cleaning service").
2. Name the **main alternative** your customer would choose if you didn't exist.
3. Write your **key difference** (from your USP) and **reason to believe** (proof).
4. Draw a 2×2 positioning map and place yourself and 3–5 alternatives.
5. Run **P-STR-025** to finalise.

## Tool 4.5 — Pricing Strategy Worksheet

**Use this when:** setting prices for a new offer, reviewing prices that feel too low, or deciding how to structure tiers and discounts.
**Time:** 45–60 minutes.
**You will produce:** W-STR-15 — a price decision supported by three reference points and a pricing test plan.

### How it works

Price sits between three reference points. The Aqvani **Price Corridor**:

```
COST FLOOR ───────────── MARKET RANGE ───────────── VALUE CEILING
(below this you lose     (what alternatives          (the most the outcome is
money)                   charge, date-checked)       worth to the customer)
```

**1. Cost floor** — the minimum price that covers your costs and target margin:

| Line | How to calculate |
|---|---|
| A. Direct cost per unit | Materials, supplies, travel, platform/payment fees, packaging, shipping per sale/job |
| B. Your time per unit | Hours per unit × your hourly cost (what you'd pay someone to do it) |
| C. Overhead share per unit | Monthly fixed costs ÷ expected units per month |
| D. Total cost per unit | A + B + C |
| E. Target margin | e.g. 40% = 0.40 (choose your own) |
| **Floor price** | **D ÷ (1 − E)** |

Check: if D = ₹1,000 and E = 0.40, floor price = 1,000 ÷ 0.60 = ₹1,667. At ₹1,667, margin = (1,667 − 1,000) ÷ 1,667 ≈ 40% ✔.

**2. Market range** — the low, typical and high prices of alternatives you collected in Layer 3. Date every check.

**3. Value ceiling** — what the result is worth to the customer: money saved or earned, time saved, risk avoided, or the cost of the next-best alternative. You will rarely price at the ceiling, but knowing it shows you how much room you have.

**Choose a pricing model:**

| Model | Good for |
|---|---|
| Per unit / per job | Products, one-off services |
| Package / bundle | Combining items to raise order value |
| Subscription / retainer | Recurring needs (maintenance, marketing, coaching) |
| Tiered (Entry / Core / Premium) | Different budgets and needs |
| Project-based | Consulting, custom work with defined scope |
| Hourly / daily | Unclear scope (best avoided where possible) |

**Discount rules** — decide in advance: when you will discount, by how much, and what the customer gives in return (longer commitment, upfront payment, referral). Never discount without a reason.

> **Tax note.** Whether prices are shown inclusive or exclusive of GST (or other taxes), and what rates apply, depends on your registration and the item. **[VERIFY CURRENT INFORMATION]** with a qualified tax professional.

### Steps

1. Calculate your cost floor.
2. List the market range from W-STR-08 (with dates).
3. Estimate the value ceiling in plain terms.
4. Choose a model and set a price within the corridor that matches your positioning.
5. Write discount rules.
6. Run **P-STR-026** to check your reasoning, and **P-STR-027** to prepare how you'll present the price.
7. Plan a pricing test: what you will measure for 30 days before changing anything.

## Tool 5.1 — Business Goal Planner

## Tool 5.2 — SMART Goal Worksheet

**Use this when:** a goal is clear in direction but vague in measurement.
**Time:** 10–15 minutes per goal.
**You will produce:** W-STR-17 — each goal rewritten as Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound, with leading indicators.

### How it works

| Letter | Question | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S — Specific** | What exactly will change? | "More customers" | "More confirmed repair bookings from repeat customers" |
| **M — Measurable** | What number shows progress? | "Grow a lot" | "From ~20 to 45 bookings/month" |
| **A — Achievable** | Is it realistic with current resources? | Doubling with no extra capacity | Within the capacity of 4 technicians |
| **R — Relevant** | Does it serve your direction? | A vanity goal | Supports the 12-month direction |
| **T — Time-bound** | By when? With which checkpoints? | "Soon" | "By 31 March, checked monthly" |

**Leading vs lagging indicators.** Revenue and bookings are *lagging* — they tell you what already happened. Add 1–2 *leading* indicators you can influence weekly (e.g. follow-ups sent, quotes issued, posts published, response time).

### Steps

1. Take one goal from W-STR-16.
2. Answer S, M, A, R and T separately.
3. Write the final SMART statement in one sentence.
4. Add 1–2 leading indicators and a weekly target for each.
5. Run **P-STR-029** to check it.

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|

| S — Specific | |
| M — Measurable (baseline → target) | |
| A — Achievable (resources/capacity) | |
| R — Relevant (link to direction) | |
| T — Time-bound (deadline + checkpoints) | |
| **Final SMART statement** | |

## Tool 5.6 — Growth Opportunity Finder

**Use this when:** your foundation is stable and you are deciding where the next growth should come from — or when growth has stalled.
**Time:** 40 minutes.
**You will produce:** W-STR-21 — eight growth routes scored, with your top two growth bets.

### How it works

There are only a handful of ways a small business grows. The Aqvani **Growth Routes** list eight, starting with the cheapest and lowest-risk:

| # | Route | Typical example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Improve conversion** — turn more existing enquiries into customers | Faster replies, better follow-up, clearer quotes |
| 2 | **Retention & repeat** — existing customers buy again | Maintenance plans, reminders, reorder prompts |
| 3 | **Average order value** — customers buy more each time | Bundles, add-ons, tiers |
| 4 | **Referrals & partnerships** — others send you customers | Referral rewards, complementary businesses |
| 5 | **New channel** — reach the same customers in a new place | Google Business Profile, marketplace, LinkedIn |
| 6 | **New offer** — sell something new to the same customers | A course, a product, a premium tier |
| 7 | **New segment** — sell the same offer to a new type of customer | Corporate clients, a different age group |
| 8 | **New location / market** — expand geographically | Neighbouring city, online-only delivery, export |

Routes 1–4 usually build on what you already have. Routes 5–8 usually need more investment and carry more risk.

**Scoring (1–5 each):**
- **Fit** — fits your strategy, strengths and positioning.
- **Evidence** — you have signs that customers want it (requests, data, repeat questions).
- **Effort** — work and money required (1 = little, 5 = a lot).
- **Risk** — what you could lose (1 = little, 5 = a lot).

**Growth score** = Fit + Evidence + (6 − Effort) + (6 − Risk) → out of 20.

Check: Fit 4, Evidence 5, Effort 2, Risk 2 → 4 + 5 + 4 + 4 = **17**.

3. Calculate the growth score.
4. Run **P-STR-034** to challenge scores and suggest ideas you missed.
5. Choose your top two growth bets. Add one first test for each to your next roadmap.

## Common Strategy Mistakes (Module-wide)

| # | Mistake | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Skipping Layer 2** | Marketing that "sounds fine" but doesn't convert | Get real customer evidence before writing USP or messaging |
| 2 | **Asking AI before thinking** | Generic outputs that could fit any business | Fill the worksheet first; paste it into the prompt |
| 3 | **Accepting AI facts** | Market sizes, competitor prices or legal claims you can't trace | Use only facts you've verified; mark the rest [VERIFY CURRENT INFORMATION] |
| 4 | **Serving everyone** | Many small, unrelated jobs; unclear message | Choose one focus niche for 90 days |
| 5 | **Competing only on price** | Discounts, thin margins, tired owner | Use the Price Corridor; compete on a provable difference |
| 6 | **Vague offers** | Scope creep, disputes, slow decisions | Use the 8-part Offer Stack with written exclusions |
| 7 | **Too many goals** | Busy every day, little progress | Max 3 goals; a "will not" list |
| 8 | **Planning 100% of time** | Constantly behind schedule | Plan 60–70% of strategic hours |
| 9 | **No review rhythm** | The strategy document is never read again | Monthly 30-minute review with P-STR-035 |
| 10 | **Strategy in your head only** | Team and AI assistant don't know the plan | Share the One-Page Strategy Summary |
