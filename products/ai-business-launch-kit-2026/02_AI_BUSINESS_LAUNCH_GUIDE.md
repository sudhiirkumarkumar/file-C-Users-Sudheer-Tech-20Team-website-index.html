## 1.2 AI Idea Validation System

Do not skip this. An idea is a guess. Validation turns a guess into a decision you can act on with confidence — not a guarantee of success, but a reasoned one.

### Business Idea Validation Worksheet

Fill in for your top 1–3 shortlisted ideas.

```
IDEA NAME: _______________________________________

1. In one sentence, what does this business do?
   _______________________________________________

2. Who exactly is the customer? (Be specific — not "small businesses," but "solo dentists in mid-size towns")
   _______________________________________________

3. What problem does it solve for them?
   _______________________________________________

4. How do you know this problem is real? (List evidence — see Demand Research Worksheet)
   _______________________________________________

5. How would they pay to solve it today? (Competitors, workarounds, DIY effort)
   _______________________________________________

6. What would you actually deliver, step by step?
   _______________________________________________

7. What could stop this from working? (Be honest)
   _______________________________________________

8. What's the smallest version you could test this week?
   _______________________________________________
```

### Problem Validation Checklist

Check each box only if you can point to real evidence, not assumption.

```
□ I can name 3+ real people/businesses who have this problem
□ I have seen this problem mentioned unprompted (forum, review, comment, conversation)
□ People currently spend time, money, or effort working around this problem
□ The problem is specific enough that a solution can be clearly described
□ I could explain this problem back to a stranger from that audience and they'd agree
□ This problem happens often enough to justify a recurring or repeat-purchase offer
```

If you can't check at least 4 of 6, go back to research before building anything.

### Customer Pain Point Finder

Use this to surface pain points from real sources before you guess.

```
Where to look:                     What to capture:
□ Reddit/forum threads in niche    → exact phrases people use to describe the problem
□ Facebook/LinkedIn groups         → recurring complaints or questions
□ App/product reviews (1–3 star)   → what's missing or frustrating
□ Competitor comment sections      → unmet needs mentioned by customers
□ Direct conversations (5+ people) → what they've tried and why it didn't work
```

Record at least 10 raw quotes before drafting your offer — you'll reuse these in Module 3 and Module 6 for copywriting.

### Demand Research Worksheet

```
1. Search volume/interest signals for this problem/solution: _______________
2. Existing paid solutions in this space (list 3+): _______________
3. Price range of existing solutions: _______________
4. Communities/groups where this audience already gathers: _______________
5. Evidence people already pay to solve this (not just complain about it): _______________
6. Is demand growing, stable, or shrinking? What tells you that? _______________
```

## Offer Frameworks

### Starter Offer
A low-commitment entry point that proves value fast.
```
Purpose: reduce risk for a first-time buyer
Scope: one narrow deliverable, fast turnaround
Price positioning: lowest tier of your offer stack
Example structure: single deliverable + 1 revision, delivered within a fixed short window
```

### Core Offer
Your primary, most-promoted offer.
```
Purpose: the main transformation you sell
Scope: the full deliverable set solving the core problem
Price positioning: mid tier, anchors your value
Example structure: multi-part deliverable + defined timeline + limited support window
```

### Premium Offer
Highest-value, highest-touch version.
```
Purpose: serve customers who want more done-for-them, faster, or with more access to you
Scope: everything in Core, plus added speed, customization, or direct access
Price positioning: top tier
Example structure: Core deliverables + priority turnaround + 1:1 strategy time + extended support
```

### Productized Service
A service sold like a product — fixed scope, fixed price, fixed process.
```
Define: exact deliverable, exact timeline, exact price, exact process steps
Avoid: open-ended scope, custom quotes per client
Why it works with AI: AI speeds up the repeatable steps, keeping your delivery time predictable
```

### Digital Product
A template, prompt pack, planner, or guide sold without ongoing delivery effort.
```
Define: the specific transformation or time saved
Format: PDF, spreadsheet, template, prompt pack
Pricing: one-time purchase, tiered by scope (see Pricing section, file 13 listing)
```

# MODULE 9 — AI AUTOMATION STARTER KIT

Beginner-friendly workflows. None of these are fully autonomous — every workflow includes a human review step on purpose. Automation should remove repetitive work, not remove your judgment.

### Workflow 1 — Lead → Form → AI Qualification → Spreadsheet
```
Trigger:        New form submission
Input:          Form fields (name, contact, need, budget/timeline if asked)
AI step:        AI scores/summarizes the lead against your criteria
Human review:   You review the AI summary before reaching out
Output:         Row added to a tracking spreadsheet with AI summary + score
Suggested tools: Form tool + spreadsheet + automation connector + AI writing tool
Setup steps:
  1. Build the intake form with the fields you need to qualify a lead
  2. Connect form submissions to a spreadsheet via an automation tool
  3. Add an AI step that summarizes/scores each submission
  4. Review new rows daily before following up
```

### Workflow 2 — Social Comment → DM → Lead Magnet → Email
```
Trigger:        Relevant comment on your content
Input:          Comment text and commenter profile
AI step:        AI drafts a personalized DM offering the lead magnet
Human review:   You approve/edit the DM before sending
Output:         DM sent, lead magnet delivered, email captured for follow-up
Suggested tools: Social platform + AI writing tool + email capture tool
Setup steps:
  1. Prepare one lead magnet (see File 07)
  2. Draft a DM template with the AI prompt below
  3. Check comments daily, use the draft as a starting point, personalize, send
  4. Add responders to your email list with consent
```

### Workflow 3 — Customer Inquiry → AI-Assisted Response → CRM
```
Trigger:        Incoming customer inquiry (email, form, DM)
Input:          Inquiry text
AI step:        AI drafts a reply based on your FAQ/offer details
Human review:   You review and send (do not auto-send to customers)
Output:         Reply sent, inquiry logged in your CRM or spreadsheet
Suggested tools: Inbox + AI writing tool + simple CRM or spreadsheet
Setup steps:
  1. Give the AI your FAQ, offer details, and tone guide as context
  2. Draft replies using the AI, always read before sending
  3. Log the inquiry and outcome in your CRM/spreadsheet
```

### Workflow 4 — Content Idea → AI Writing → Review → Publishing
```
Trigger:        New content idea (from calendar or inspiration)
Input:          Topic, format, target platform
AI step:        AI drafts the content using your brand voice/prompts
Human review:   You fact-check, personalize, and approve before posting
Output:         Published content
Suggested tools: AI writing tool + your brand voice guide (Module 4) + scheduling tool
Setup steps:
  1. Pull the day's topic from your content calendar (File 07)
  2. Run the relevant Prompt Vault content prompt
  3. Edit for accuracy and voice, then schedule/post
```

### Workflow 5 — New Customer → Welcome Email → Onboarding Checklist
```
Trigger:        New customer/purchase confirmed
Input:          Customer name, product/service purchased
AI step:        AI drafts a personalized welcome email and onboarding checklist
Human review:   You review before the email sends (or spot-check if automated)
Output:         Welcome email sent, onboarding checklist delivered
Suggested tools: Email platform + AI writing tool + automation connector
Setup steps:
  1. Draft a welcome email template with the AI, covering what happens next
  2. Build a simple onboarding checklist for the customer
  3. Connect purchase confirmation to trigger the email send
  4. Spot-check periodically to make sure it still reads well
```
